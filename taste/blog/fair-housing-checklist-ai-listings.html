A fair housing checklist for AI-written listing copy

By T, Steele Software, Raleigh

AI can draft listing copy in seconds, and it can also slip in wording a licensed agent would never write. Run these checks on every draft before it goes out.

The federal Fair Housing Act protects people from discrimination based on race, color, religion, sex, national origin, familial status and disability, and North Carolina’s State Fair Housing Act covers the same groups. Advertising that suggests a preference for or against any of them is a problem, whoever or whatever wrote it.

The checklist

  1. Describe the property, never the buyer. “Three bedrooms and a fenced yard,” not “perfect for a young family.”
  2. No words about who belongs. Cut anything about the kind of people, neighbors or community a buyer would “fit” with.
  3. Nothing about religion or national origin. Name a nearby place by its plain name and distance if it helps, without suggesting who it suits.
  4. Accessibility features stated as facts. “Step-free entrance and a roll-in shower” is a feature. Never imply who can or cannot live there.
  5. No sex or age preferences. Drop phrases like “bachelor pad” or “ideal for empty nesters.”
  6. Check every fact. AI can invent features, schools or distances. Compare the draft with the listing sheet line by line.
  7. Check captions and emails too. The same rules apply to social posts, flyers and “Just Listed” emails.
  8. A licensed agent approves it. Nothing AI writes goes live without your review.

Build it into the prompt

Add this line to every listing prompt so the first draft is closer to right:

Describe the property and its features only. Do not describe or suggest any type of buyer, household, neighbor or community.

It reduces problems. It does not replace your review.

This is general guidance, not legal advice. For a specific question, ask your broker or a real estate attorney.

Set up a daily morning brief from Gmail and Google Calendar

By T, Steele Software, Raleigh

A morning brief turns your inbox and calendar into five lines: what is on today, what needs a reply and what matters most. You read it with your coffee instead of digging through email.

What you need

  • A paid Claude or ChatGPT plan. Connecting Google apps is a paid feature on both, and the details change, so check the current settings.
  • Gmail and Google Calendar connected in the app’s settings, under connectors or apps.

The prompt

Look at my Google Calendar for today and my Gmail from the last 24 hours.
Give me:
1. Today's appointments and showings, with times and addresses.
2. Emails that need a reply from me today, one line each, most urgent first.
3. Any deadlines in the next 3 days mentioned in my email.
4. My top three priorities for today.
Keep it under 150 words. Do not send, archive or delete anything.

Make it a habit

  1. Save the prompt in a project named “Morning brief” so it is one click away.
  2. Run it at the same time each morning, or schedule it if your plan offers scheduled tasks.
  3. After a week, adjust it. Add a section for new leads, or drop what you never read.

Keep it safe

  • The brief only reads. Keep “do not send, archive or delete anything” in the prompt.
  • Double-check any deadline it finds against the contract itself.
  • Do not paste client financial details into chats you share with anyone.
